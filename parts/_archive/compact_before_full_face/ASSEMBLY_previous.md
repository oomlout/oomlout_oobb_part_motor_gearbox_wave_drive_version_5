# Compact 12:1 assembly sequence

Use the current 12:1 print pack, not the archived 20:1 opposed-bank parts. Print one of each of the eleven designs, or the two provided 220 x 220 mm beds. Purchase twelve 7 x 12 mm nylon spacers, one 6810 and one 6704 bearing. Hardware quantities are in `hardware.yaml`. The motor is a purchased 40 mm NEMA 17 with the reference shaft and pilot dimensions described in README.

1. Print and check the bearing, journal, D-shaft, nylon-slot and nut coupons. Clear only the intended OOBB bridge skins in nut/socket pockets. Check each printed nut seat for intact material. No preload or load rating is established for these plastic parts.
2. Seat the motor pilot in the underside of `motor_mount_plate`. Fit four M3 x 6 socket caps from above into the motor's 31 mm mounting pattern. Confirm the assumed 5.2 mm thread penetration does not bottom in your motor.
3. Preload four M2 x 16 socket caps upward into `cage_lower_cap` before placing it on the motor plate. Their heads sit in the underside recesses. Align the four bolt positions with the motor-plate hex-key access holes (15, 105, 195 and 285 degrees). The cap sits 0.3 mm clear of the stationary plate.
4. Insert the M3 nut into the bottom-loading slot in `motor_eccentric`. Slip the eccentric down the motor D shaft through the lower cage cap, setting its bottom at Z=4.3 mm relative to the motor face. Tighten its M3 x 8 grub screw onto the flat while the fixed ring and rollers are absent. The lower cage cap must go on first: it will not pass over the eccentric's larger offset shoulder.
5. Load four M3 nuts into `wave_generator` through the bearing-cap pocket. Seat the 6704 in the wave wheel. Lower this assembly over the eccentric journal. Fit the upper eccentric collar with its nut already loaded, leave the designed 0.1 mm clearance above the bearing inner race, and tighten its M3 x 8 grub on the shaft flat. The collar is 6.8 mm tall, leaving 1.8 mm of the reference motor shaft above it.
6. Fit `wave_bearing_cap` around the collar and close with four M3 x 6 socket caps. The bearing outer race is retained by the wheel and cap; the inner race is retained by the eccentric shoulders. Avoid clamping away the intended clearance.
7. Lower the fixed profile ring around this assembly onto the motor plate, aligning the six case screw holes. At the zero reference pose the eccentric centre is displaced +X by 0.75 mm and the motor shaft flat faces +X. Insert the twelve nylon rollers axially into the wave/profile space.
8. Load the four output M3 nuts into the underside-facing pockets in `output_cage` before lowering the cage over the rollers. The cage is inverted relative to its printing orientation: the broad floor is above the rollers, and its fingers point down. Align its radial windows with the rollers and its four M2 holes with the preloaded lower-cap screws. Load the four M2 nuts into the exposed top pockets. Tighten the M2 screws through the motor-plate access holes with an L hex key. The access axes clear the front motor body; route the key around the rear connector/cable.
9. Fit `output_hub` on the cage floor, aligning its four holes. Fit the 6810 around the hub. This is the only large output bearing; there is no lower 6810 or extra input support bearing.
10. Fit `output_bearing_housing` with the bearing mouth downward, opposite its printing orientation. Its upper shoulder and the fixed ring's lower lip retain the outer race with axial clearance. Load six M3 nuts in the top pockets, then install six M3 x 30 socket caps upward from the motor-plate underside. Their locations lie outside the motor body.
11. Fit `output_retainer` and its four M3 x 12 caps into the cage-floor nuts. Its 30 mm square mounting pattern is available for the output attachment. The central hole remains clear of the motor shaft and eccentric collar.
12. Turn the motor shaft slowly by hand. Confirm free motion of all twelve rollers, free output rotation, no axial binding, and approximately one reverse output turn per twelve input turns. Recheck fastener seating and bearing retention before considering powered testing.

The circular body is 79 mm OD; the rounded square plate is 83 mm wide. Overall gearbox height above the motor face is 35.1 mm. Exploded drawings use illustrative separation, not assembly dimensions. In a single bank the eccentric is not counterbalanced. Actual motor bearing capacity, output moment loading and printed-part strength require testing; the drawing does not establish a safe operating torque or speed.
